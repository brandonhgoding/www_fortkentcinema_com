THE PITCH

Play a silent film. Play a rock record. Start them at the same moment and the two stop being separate things.

That is Silents Synced, a touring series from Josh Frank of Austin’s Blue Starlite drive-in. Frank matches a forgotten silent picture to an album that turns out to have been arguing with it all along, then screens the two together. Earlier pairings put Nosferatu against Radiohead’s Kid A and Amnesiac, and Buster Keaton’s Sherlock Jr. against R.E.M.’s Monster.

For the third installment he took a 1925 picture about a countess who refuses to behave, and handed it to {{ event.artist }}.

She’s punk rock before punk even existed.

Josh Frank, creator of Silents Synced

Frank has said the pairing arrived on a single line: “The moment I rewatched this film, I remembered the lyrics of ‘Animal’ — ‘I’d rather be with an animal’ — it just fit.”

THE PICTURE

{{ event.title }} turns one hundred this year. Pola Negri plays a European countess who lands in a small Midwestern town with a blunt manner, a sharp tongue, and a tattoo on her arm — and scandalizes the place simply by declining to be made smaller by it. Frank’s reading of her is the reason the record fits.
