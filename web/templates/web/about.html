OUR STORY

Maine’s northernmost picture show — bringing the big screen to the St. John Valley since 1917.

{% comment %} How it started {% endcomment %}

HOW IT STARTED

Our story begins with an old barn. In the early 1900s a group of local investors tied to the Modern Woodmen of America bought it, and by 1917 they were showing moving pictures inside — and the Savoy Theater was born. In those early days the Savoy stood on the very lot where Fort Kent’s post office sits today, a gathering place where Valley families first watched the world flicker to life on a screen.

In 1969, as Fort Kent celebrated its 100th birthday, a brand-new theater rose right next door on Hall Street. The old Savoy came down and the Century Theater took its place — the same beloved picture show, rebuilt for a new generation. For decades the Century was cared for by Ben Paradis, who kept it going through every change the movie business could throw at it. He brought in digital projection, and with the help of a grant he divided the single grand auditorium into the two cozy screens you’ll find today.

When the world went dark in 2020, the Century closed its doors for the first time in its history. It reopened in 2021, and in 2024 — after the community rallied to keep the lights on — it began a new chapter under new local ownership as Fort Kent Cinema. Same seats, same balcony, same small-town magic: a new name carrying more than a century of stories forward.

{% comment %} Mission {% endcomment %}

OUR MISSION

Fort Kent is a long way from the nearest big-city multiplex — and that is exactly why this place matters. Fort Kent Cinema exists to keep the big-screen experience alive and affordable right here in the St. John Valley: a warm, welcoming spot where neighbors run into neighbors, kids see their very first movie, and a Friday night out doesn’t mean a two-hour drive. We’re proud to be Maine’s northernmost picture show — and prouder still to be yours.
